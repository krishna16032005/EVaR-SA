\section{The algorithm in one page}

Standard SAC, with two substitutions: the critic returns a \emph{distribution} over
returns instead of a scalar, and the actor maximises a \emph{risk functional} of
that distribution instead of its mean.

\vspace{-0.2em}
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white,colframe=soft!30,boxrule=0.5pt,left=6pt,right=6pt,top=4pt,bottom=4pt]
\small
\textbf{Risk functional (the only genuinely new part).} For return samples $Z$ and
level $\alpha$:
\vspace{-0.4em}
\begin{equation*}
\evar_\alpha[Z] = \min_{x>0} G(x), \qquad
G(x) = x\Big(\log \mathbb{E}\big[e^{Z/x}\big] - \log\alpha\Big), \qquad x = 1/\beta .
\end{equation*}
\vspace{-1.0em}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=0.1em,topsep=0.2em]
\item $G$ is strongly convex $\Rightarrow$ $G'$ monotone $\Rightarrow$
      \textbf{bisect $G'$ in $\log x$}. 20 steps. Never Newton (see \S4).
\item Search interval \textbf{per row}: $[10^{-3}\,\mathrm{sd}(Z),\;
      10^{3}\,\mathrm{sd}(Z)]$. Not a constant (see \S4).
\item \textbf{Detach $x^\ast$.} Envelope theorem gives exact gradients without
      unrolling the solver.
\item If $p_{\max}\ge\alpha$, return $\max(Z)$ exactly --- no interior optimum exists.
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{-0.4em}
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\small
\caption{DSAC-EVaR update (one env step $=$ one update)}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Sample minibatch $(s,a,r,c,s',d)$ from replay; \quad $r_{\text{eff}} \gets r - \lambda c$
\State $a' \sim \pi_\phi(\cdot|s')$ \Comment{tanh-squashed Gaussian}
\State $y_j \gets r_{\text{eff}} + \gamma(1-d)\big(\min_{k\in\{1,2\}} Z^{\text{targ}}_{k,\tau_j}(s',a') - \eta\log\pi_\phi(a'|s')\big)$
       \Comment{$d$ = \textbf{terminated} only}
\State $\mathcal{L}_Z \gets \sum_{k}\mathrm{QuantileHuber}\big(Z_k(s,a),\, y\big)$;\quad update both critics
\State $\tilde a \sim \pi_\phi(\cdot|s)$;\quad $z \gets \big[Z_1(s,\tilde a);\, Z_2(s,\tilde a)\big]$
       \Comment{stack $(2B,K)$: one solve, not two}
\State $\rho \gets \rho_\alpha(z)$ \Comment{$\evar$ via bisection, or mean/$\cvar$/Wang/\dots}
\State $\mathcal{L}_\pi \gets \mathbb{E}\big[\eta\log\pi_\phi(\tilde a|s) - \min_k \rho_k\big]$;\quad update actor
\State $\mathcal{L}_\eta \gets -\eta\big(\log\pi_\phi(\tilde a|s) + \bar{\mathcal{H}}\big)$;\quad update temperature,
       $\bar{\mathcal{H}} = -\dim(\mathcal{A})$
\State Polyak-update targets, $\tau = 0.005$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\vspace{-0.6em}
\textbf{The one structural requirement: the critic must be $Z(s,a)$, not $Z(s)$.}
$\evar$ is translation-equivariant, so $\evar(r + Z(s')) \equiv r + \evar(Z(s'))$
for a \emph{sampled scalar} $r$ --- the tilt never touches the reward, and at a
terminal step $\alpha$ drops out entirely. Measured exact regret: $86.35\%$ with
the state-value form, $0.00\%$ with the action-value form. Everything else here is
a detail; this one is the method.

\textbf{Swap the risk functional, keep everything else.} \texttt{--risk mean} is the
risk-neutral control sharing critic, actor, data and seeds. Run it every time: it is
the only thing that lets you attribute a difference to the risk measure rather than
to the algorithm. Also available: \texttt{cvar}, \texttt{wang}, \texttt{cpw},
\texttt{entropic} (fixed $\beta$), \texttt{meanvar}.

\textbf{Why the ties are not failures.} Pendulum's return spread is policy noise,
not risk in the world. On SafetyPointGoal1 the measured spread of the critic's
distribution is $\mathrm{sd}(Z)\approx 0.022$ against episode returns near $27$ ---
\textbf{under 1\%}. With no spread there is no tail to trade, so every risk attitude
ties by construction. Both operator health checks pass in the same runs
(\texttt{at\_bound\_frac}$=0$, \texttt{top\_mass\_frac}$=0.017\approx 1/K$), which is
what makes that conclusion safe rather than a guess.

\textbf{Throughput} (A40, batch 256): Pendulum $\evar$ 75 steps/s vs \texttt{mean}
112; SafetyPointGoal1 49 vs 63. MuJoCo itself runs at 617 steps/s, so the learner is
always the bottleneck --- the EVaR solve costs $12\%$ on CPU, the rest is GPU
kernel-launch overhead.

\section{Six things that will bite you}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item \textbf{$\alpha > 1/K$, always.} The top of $K$ samples carries mass $1/K$;
      once that reaches $\alpha$ the dual runs to $x_{\min}$ and $\evar$ collapses
      onto $\max(Z)$. At $K=64$ the floor is $\alpha>0.0156$. Binds independently on
      the evaluation sample size.
\item \textbf{Never a fixed $[x_{\min},x_{\max}]$.} $x^\ast$ has \emph{units of
      return} ($x^\ast\approx 0.34\,\mathrm{sd}(Z)$). A fixed interval works at one
      return scale and silently turns $\evar$ into
      $\text{mean} + x_{\min}\log(1/\alpha)$ --- fixed-$\beta$ entropic utility,
      i.e.\ your own baseline. Measured error up to $1100\%$ at
      $\mathrm{sd}(Z)=10^{-3}$.
\item \textbf{Bisection, not Newton.} When the tilted measure collapses onto one
      atom, $G''=\mathrm{Var}_{Q_x}/x^3$ underflows, the step explodes, projects onto
      a bound, and cycles forever. Newton returned a \emph{constant} $x^\ast=0.301$
      across different distributions and inflated $\evar$ by $25$--$590\%$.
\item \textbf{Mask zero-probability atoms to $-\infty$; do not
      \texttt{clamp\_min(1e-12)}.} The tilt multiplies invented mass by
      $e^{z_{\max}/x}$ --- a factor of $e^{45}$ in a realistic case, worth $+13.3\%$
      on $\evar$.
\item \textbf{Size a categorical support in \emph{discounted} units} if you use
      C51 at all. Undiscounted episode return left $\approx 9$ of $51$ atoms
      carrying mass. IQN avoids the problem entirely --- prefer it.
\item \textbf{Leave \texttt{MUJOCO\_GL} unset} for headless Safety-Gymnasium
      training. EGL or OSMesa in the same process as a CUDA context segfaults
      (rc 139). Lidar observations need no GL. Also: safety-gymnasium pins
      \texttt{gymnasium 0.28.1}, so it needs its own venv \emph{with its own CUDA
      torch build}.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Instrument all of this.} Log \texttt{at\_bound\_frac} ($0$ for $\alpha<1$),
\texttt{top\_mass\_frac} (near $1/K$), and \texttt{z\_sd\_mean} (says whether the
environment can support the experiment \emph{before} you spend a day on it). Each
was added after the fault it detects had already cost real time. Validate the
diagnostics themselves against known answers --- our first bound-check compared the
solution to itself and reported every solve as broken.

\section{What remains: benchmarks and baselines}

setting where an \emph{adaptive} tilt beats a fixed one, plus the standard
comparisons. Framed as an agent $\times$ task $\times$ constraint matrix, in the
